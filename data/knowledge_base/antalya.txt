Antalya is a city on the Turkish Mediterranean coast and the chief resort of the "Turkish Riviera". It is a metropolis with a population of 2.7 million, the fifth largest city in Turkey. The city was named for its first documented ruler Attalos II (220–138 BC), king of Pergamon, though it was obviously settled much earlier. Attaleia grew up around what is now called the Roman harbour. It never grew large initially as it lacked a hinterland, hemmed in by mountains with difficult routes inland, although a fertile coastal plain extends to the east. Early visitors included St Paul the Apostle and Ibn Battuta, who in 1331 AD recorded how Christians, Greeks, Jews, and the royal court each inhabited their own walled enclave, while the Muslims lived in the main body of town within an enclosing wall.

This relative isolation meant little fighting here, but Antalya was the prize to be handed over after conquests elsewhere: to the Seljuks in 1207 and the Ottomans in 1391. After Turkey's defeat in World War I, the harsh 1920 Treaty of Sèvres handed over tracts of the country to the Allies, and Italy gained Antalya. This was reversed by the 1923 Treaty of Lausanne. In the 1970s, commercial airliners could jet tourists non-stop from northern Europe, and the political environment boosted tourist infrastructure. The city burgeoned with development on either side of Kaleiçi, the old town centre, and especially east along the sandy Lara beach. Effectively this strip extends 150 km along the coast.

The climate is Mediterranean. Summers are hot, sunny and humid yet rainless. Days average 30–35°C and may surpass 40°C; nights can be muggy and sticky, so air conditioning is essential. Winters are mostly mild, punctuated by storms, downpours and strong winds. April–May and October–November are optimum conditions for sightseeing, and the sea remains warm late into autumn.

Hadrian's Gate (Turkish: Üçkapılar, "three gates") is a landmark starting point for exploring Kaleiçi, the old quarter. Built in 130 AD for the visit of Emperor Hadrian, it may have been ceremonial rather than a practical thoroughfare, as it became bricked up within the city walls. This preserved it, and it was only rediscovered when the walls crumbled in the 1950s.

The Fluted Minaret Mosque (Yivliminare Camii) on Cumhuriyet Cd is an elegant six-domed mosque built under Seljuk rule in 1230 and rebuilt in 1373 by the Hamidids. The minaret is 38 m tall. Opposite the mosque, the Dervish Lodge Museum used to be the home of the Mevlevihane, the "Whirling Dervishes," and is open Tuesday–Sunday 08:30–17:30, free of charge. The Clocktower (Saat Kulesi) just east of the mosque dates from the 9th century AD. Tekeli Mehmet Paşa Mosque, facing the clocktower, was built in the 17th century and is named for a military commander who was appointed Grand Vizier in 1595.

Sehzade Korkut Mosque (Broken Minaret Mosque), on Civlek Sk, was first built as a Roman temple in the 2nd century AD and in later centuries went back and forth between religions. The minaret was added in the early 13th century, and in 1800 it was the only part to survive a fire. The mosque lay in ruins until rebuilt from 2018, reopening in 2021.

The Toy Museum is by the harbour and is open Tuesday–Sunday 09:00–18:00, free of charge. The small Iskele Mosque by the harbour is compact, almost a kiosk. The harbour piers offer pleasant views over Old Town and the sunset. The Ethnographic Museum on Civelek Sk 20, on the south side of the harbour, exhibits traditional arts and crafts daily 08:30–17:30, free of charge.

Hıdırlık Tower on Hıdırlık Sk was built by the Romans in the 2nd century AD with a square base and circular upper storey. It served as a bastion and lighthouse and is free to visit. Atatürk House Museum on Fevzi Çakmak Cd 11 is open daily 08:30–17:30 and shows memorabilia from Atatürk's stays in Antalya in 1930, 1931 and 1935, free of charge. Murat Paşa Mosque on Milli Egemenlik Cd was commissioned in 1570 and has a high dome and beautiful Seljuk calligraphy within.

Antalya Museum on Konyaaltı Cd 88, reachable by tram to the Müze stop, is one of Turkey's largest museums with 5,000 items illustrating the history of the Mediterranean and Pamphylia regions. It also stores some 25,000 items not on display. Opening hours are April–September daily 08:30–20:00, October–March Tuesday–Sunday 08:30–18:00. Adult admission is 340 TL.

Antalya Aquarium on Dumlupınar Blv 502 in Konyaaltı is a huge aquarium with display tanks and a long viewing tunnel. It is open daily 09:00–23:00 with adult or child admission at 950 TL.

The Düden Lower Waterfalls on Lara Cd are where the Düden River plunges 40 m dramatically into the sea. Further upstream, the Düden Upper Waterfalls are where the river emerges from karstic tunnels to fall 20 m. Sandland (Kum Heykel Müzesi) on Lara Cd is a sand sculpture park open year round, lit up at night, with adult admission at 250 TL and child admission at 125 TL.

Tünek Tepe, on Kemer Yolu in Konyaaltı, is a hilltop of 618 m with great views over the city and coast. You can drive or hike up, though the cable car collapsed in April 2024.

Perge, near Aksu, is an extensive ruin of a city that had its heyday in the 2nd to 3rd century AD under the Romans. The site has a colonnaded agora, theatre, stadium, temple to Artemis and two churches. It is open daily 08:00–19:30, adult admission 250 TL.

Kurşunlu Waterfall, northeast of Antalya on D685 near Aksu, is a woodland park with impressive waterfalls that dry to a trickle in summer. A firm path leads past the best views. There are refreshment stalls and a café/restaurant. It is open daily 08:30–19:30.

King's Pool (Kral Havuzu) is an attractive free pool in a canyon.

Sillyon was a fortified city that had its heyday in the Byzantine era from about 680 to 820 AD. The ruins are scattered down the hillside.

Aspendos, 40 km east near Serik, is a Graeco-Roman city whose centrepiece is a well-preserved amphitheatre seating 7,000. There is also 2 km of aqueduct (which siphoned water over rises), a stadium, baths, basilica, agora and nymphaeum. About 1 km downstream near the modern highway is the Eurymedon Bridge, rebuilt in the 13th century when the Roman original washed away. It is open daily 08:00–17:00, adult admission 340 TL.

Zeytintaşı Cave is a small show-cave near Akbaş in the mountains above Aspendos, visited by guided tour.

Boat trips leave from the harbour and are a popular summer activity. Aqua parks include Waterhill opposite the Aquarium, and Park Aqua, Wet & Wild and Trendy Lara along the Lara beach strip. The Golden Orange Film Festival is held in October. The Lycian Way, a renowned long-distance hiking trail, stretches from Fethiye in the west to the mountains above Antalya in the east, passing through the climbing centre of Geyikbayırı.

For shopping, MarkAntalya AVM is the main shopping centre, 200 m east of Murat Paşa Mosque, open daily 10:00–23:00. Migros supermarkets are commonly found around town, and there is also Migros AVM (5M Migros) on Atatürk Blv, a shopping centre with a large Migros alongside many other stores. TerraCity on Tekelioglu Cd 55 in Fener is a shopping centre on the east side of the city toward Lara, with some 180 stores and several restaurants. Numerous farmers' markets operate around Antalya, especially in Konyaaltı.

Budget dining options cluster around the downtown streets near Muratpaşa Mosque. Piyazcı Sami on 25th Sk 1 serves Antalya-style piyaz (bean salad), köfte and other authentic fare daily 10:00–22:00. Özkan Kebap on 1253th Sk 8/A has a good menu selection and is popular with locals, open daily 07:00–22:00. Çiğköfteci Ömer Usta on Anafartalar Cd 65 is a friendly place for çiğ köfte (raw meatballs), open daily 11:00–01:00. Gizem Cafe Fast Food on Milli Egemenlik Cd is inexpensive for traditional shawarma and dürüm. In Konyaaltı, Public Konyaaltı on Akdeniz Blv serves burgers, thin-crust pizza and filling food daily 12:00–02:00, and Yamaçoba on Atatürk Blv offers great quality inexpensive food at the west end of the beach strip.

For mid-range dining, Rokka Pizza Falafel on Recep Peker Cd opposite Hadrian's Gate has lots of vegetarian and vegan choices, open daily 11:00–23:00. Ayar Meyhanesi on Hesapçı Sk 59 is a friendly restaurant with a great selection of fresh fish, open daily 12:00–01:00. Grand Friends on Kocatepe Sk 37 serves fish and steak to a high standard with friendly service. Hasanağa on Mescit Sk 15 is a long-established family-run restaurant with a pleasant garden setting and great traditional food, open daily 11:00–02:00. Karaf Bistro Kaleiçi on Sakarya Sk 14 is a pleasant relaxing place with pizza and wine, open Monday–Saturday 14:00–00:00. For fine dining, Seraser on Paşa Cami Sk 14 is open daily 16:00–00:00.

Antalya has a lively nightlife scene. The old town and city centre are filled with bars. Names like Sheffield Pub, Edinburgh Social House and Dubh Linn cater to a British and Irish demographic. The Konyaaltı beach strip to the west has The James Joyce and The Barrels. Lara town centre has Lucky Sports Bar and Patrick Irish Pub, though on the beach strip further east the hotel bars dominate. Doluca Vineyard is located next to the airport entrance.

Kaleiçi, the core of Old Town, and Kılınçarslan, its southern extension, have many inexpensive pansiyons. Whitegarden Hotel on Hesapçı Geçidi Sk 9 is a gorgeous little place at around 2,500 TL per night for a B&B double. Sibel Pansiyon on Fırın Sk 30 is a welcoming hotel with aircon, satellite TV and private bathrooms, quiet at night with good breakfasts, around 1,500 TL per night. Hotel Blue Sea Garden on Hesapçı Sk 65, next to Hıdırlık Tower, has a garden restaurant and rooms with sea views, around 1,500 TL. Alp Paşa Hotel on Hesapçı Sk 30 in Kaleiçi is an atmospheric hotel with a good restaurant, around 1,000 TL. Tuvana Hotel on Karanlık Sk 18 in Kaleiçi is a peaceful hotel near the centre with 45 rooms and a small pool, around 2,000 TL. The DoubleTree by Hilton on Adnan Menderes Blv 61 is a reliable mid-range chain hotel at the north end of downtown, around 2,000 TL.

Konyaaltı is southwest of the centre with 10 km of pebble beach and some industry around the marina at its west end. Western package holidays don't base here, so prices tend to be lower than in Lara. Hotel Su on Dumlupınar Blv 205 near the Aquarium has quirky decor and good service, around 2,500 TL. Megasaray Westbeach on Akdeniz Blv 192 gets great reviews as a beachfront hotel, around 2,500 TL.

The Lara hotel strip stretches east for 20 km. Nearest to town is Şirinyalı, which is bustling but has limited beach, so prices are lower. Rates increase along the long sandy beach east from Kundu to Aksu, which is prime territory for western package deals. Hotels have more room to spread out further east. Akra Hotels on Lara Cd 24 is a smart efficient hotel, around 3,000 TL. Holiday Inn Lara is 1 km from the beach and offers good value at around 2,000 TL. Lara Barut on Lara Turizm Yolu gets great reviews as an upmarket family-oriented hotel, around 9,000 TL. Delphin Imperial on Lara Turizm Yolu gets great reviews for comfort and service, around 8,000 TL. Concorde De Luxe Resort on Lara Turizm Yolu has consistently good reviews, around 4,000 TL. Grand Park Lara offers good value with B&B doubles around 3,000 TL. Aska Lara Resort is family-oriented with lots of children's activities, around 4,000 TL. Trendy Lara in Aksu is a huge family-oriented hotel at around 6,000 TL. Titanic Mardan Palace in Aksu gets great scores for comfort and service, with a huge swimming pool.

Antalya-style piyaz, a white bean salad dressed with tahini, is a local specialty. Çiğ köfte (raw spiced meatballs, often served in a wrap with greens and lemon) is widely available. Kebabs of all varieties—lamb, chicken and mixed—are ubiquitous, with many small neighborhood restaurants offering them at budget-friendly prices. Traditional dürüm wraps and shawarma are popular quick meals throughout the city.

For day trips and nearby destinations, Kemer to the southwest is part of the Antalya tourist strip, and further south along the rugged coast of Lycia you will find quiet beaches and village harbours. Belek to the east has golf links and luxury hotels. Manavgat further east has waterfalls, and the big draw is the Roman ruins at Side. Alanya, yet further east, has an ancient citadel perched on a crag with beach hotels stretching either side. Termessos is a ruined ancient city above the forests of the Taurus Mountains, reachable as a day trip from Antalya.
