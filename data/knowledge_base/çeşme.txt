Çeşme (pronounced chaysh-may) is a beach resort in the Central Aegean region of Turkey, at the tip of a peninsula 85 km west of İzmir and only 10 km from the Greek island of Chios. It is the largest town in a municipality taking in several smaller resorts and villages, which in 2022 had a population of 48,924.

The name Çeşme means "spring" or "fountain," likely referring to the geothermal hot springs on the coast a couple of km out of town. In the Middle Ages it was a more important port than İzmir, trading with the Genoese who held nearby islands such as Chios. It surrendered to the Ottomans in 1566, and trade thereafter shifted to İzmir. The town continued with trade and agriculture until the big growth from tourism in the 1970s. Much tourism is domestic, as international tourists mostly bypass Çeşme for the resorts of Marmaris and Bodrum further south.

Çeşme's old town has many 19th and early 20th century Ottoman buildings and charming back alleys around the castle and lower part of Atatürk Boulevard. Ancient artifacts dating back 2500 years have been found in the area, though no trace of the ancient city remains.

Çeşme Castle was originally built by the Genoese, then rebuilt in 1508 under Sultan Bayezit II after the town was twice attacked by Venice. The small museum within explains the town's history, and visitors can wander round the battlements. Entry is 100 TL. In front of the castle stands a monument to Cezayirli Gazi Hasan Paşa (1730–90), a captain in the 1770 naval battle in the bay who rose to become admiral and grand vizier.

Ayios Haralambos is a former Greek Orthodox church on Atatürk Boulevard, 100 m northeast of the castle. Built in 1832, it is now an art gallery and cultural venue.

Beaches are a major draw in Çeşme. Dilaila is south towards Çiftlik, and Altınkum is further south across the headland. Boyalık is the closest beach on the Ilıca strip, with several more along the peninsula to the north. The beaches are windy and well-suited for windsurfing and kiteboarding.

Thermal springs are another highlight. Çeşme means "spring" and Ilıca means "spa," and that is where you find them. Some hot springs bubble out in the sea, while others feed hotel spas along the resort strip.

Kumru is a popular local warm wrap with salami, garlic-flavoured sausage (sucuk), grilled cheese and tomatoes, also available in a vegetarian version. Atatürk Boulevard is the main eating strip, with restaurants including İmren, Şark, Seyir Et, Friendly Corner, Hülya'nın Yeri Et, Evim, Levent, and Meydan. The north waterfront has Asucan, Carpe Diem, Rihtim, Çeşme Deniz, Yücel Balık, and Miss.ina. Çeşme Marina has Fuente, Ferdi Baba and Meyhane Şerefe. In Alaçatı village, top picks are Fava, Leblebili Meyhane and İmren, with plenty more south by the marina. Dondurma is the local ice cream — try the balbadem (honey and almond) and the blackcurrant.

Nightlife options in town centre include MelBu, Star Bar, Valentino and Kırmızı, where the distinction between cafe and pub is not always obvious.

Ertan Hotel on the waterfront at Cumhuriyet Meydanı is a slick clean place with B&B doubles from 2000 TL. Rıdvan Hotel on the north side of the castle offers B&B doubles from 1500 TL. Kanuni Kervansaray just south of the castle is a 500-year-old caravanserai, now a very basic hotel. Antik Rıdvan Hotel by the marina has B&B doubles from 1000 TL. Ilıca Hotel on the beachfront is a smart efficient hotel with spa and wellness facilities, offering B&B doubles from 2500 TL. Radisson Blu Resort, 200 m west of Ilıca Hotel, has two swimming pools and spa with B&B doubles from 2000 TL. Nill's Stone House in Çiftlik is a charming friendly pansiyon a short walk to the waterfront.

Alaçatı is an attractive large village nearby with traditional architecture, cobbled streets, cascades of bougainvillea and a photogenic windmill, plus a marina and water sports facilities. İzmir is a bustling modern city with good museums. Chios is the Greek island visible from Çeşme, an hour by ferry and an easy day trip. Ephesus to the south is a must-see, an extensive and well-preserved Roman city.
