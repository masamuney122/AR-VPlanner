Bodrum is a popular tourist resort on the Southern Aegean coast of Turkey. The city sits on a large peninsula, and "Bodrum" in tourist literature covers the city center and its westward extension through Gümbet, Bitez, and Yahşi, as well as resort villages scattered along the coastline. While Bodrum has evolved into a budget, mass-market destination, this means good visitor facilities, competitive prices, and easy access to quiet coves and ancient ruins just a short trip away.

Bodrum was founded as the Grecian city of Halikarnassos. Its most famous ruler was Mausolos, the regional governor of Caria in the 4th century BC, who commissioned a tomb so grand it became one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and gave us the word "mausoleum." The tomb stood 45 meters high and was richly decorated, though earthquakes and opportunistic locals gradually dismantled it over the centuries — several prize pieces even ended up in the British Museum. The Knights Hospitaller arrived in 1402 and built Bodrum Castle using stone from the mausoleum, calling it Petronium ("St Peter's fortress"), which became "Bodrum." For centuries the town was a sleepy fishing and sponge-diving village until writer Cevat Şakir Kabaağaçlı, known as the Fisherman of Halicarnassus, popularized the area through his lyrical descriptions and "blue voyages" on traditional gulets. European tourists followed, and Bodrum transformed into the bustling resort it is today.

The climate is typically Mediterranean, with hot sunny summers and mild rainy winters. The combination of climate and popularity gives Bodrum a longer holiday season than other Turkish resorts. May is ideal for shore-based activities, while June to October is better for water sports, when sea temperatures rise above 24°C.

Bodrum Castle, also known as the Castle of St Peter, is a well-preserved bastion built from 1402 by the Knights Hospitaller. Its towers are named the English, French, German, and Italian for the four nations that constructed them — which explains why it took most of a century to complete; the Pope guaranteed its builders a place in heaven but neglected to set a deadline. Since 1962 the castle has housed the Museum of Underwater Archaeology, displaying remarkable finds from local shipwrecks. The standout is the Uluburun wreck from the 14th century BC, a late Bronze Age vessel laden with copper ingots offering a rare insight into ancient trade. Open daily, summer hours 09:00–22:30, winter 08:30–17:30. Adult admission approximately €23.

The Mausoleum of Halikarnassos at Turgut Reis Cd 93 is the site of the legendary tomb of Mausolos. Before his death in 353 BC, he commissioned a burial monument so magnificent it became one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. His wife Artemisia ruled for two more years before being interred with him in the still-unfinished structure — legend made her an icon of grieving widowhood, supposedly mixing his ashes into her drink, though the legends seldom pointed out she was also his sister. Earthquakes shook the tomb to bits by 1494 AD, or at least that's what folk said when its masonry somehow ended up in their own mansions. Today visitors explore the foundations, with exhibits and a video presentation. Open daily 08:30–17:30; adult admission approximately €3.

The Bodrum Amphitheatre on Kıbrıs Şehitleri Cd was begun by Mausolos in the 4th century BC but took its present form under the Romans in the 2nd century AD. Restored in the 1970s and 90s, it now serves as an event space seating 10,000 and hosts the annual Ballet Festival in July and August. Entry is free.

Myndos Gate is the only survivor of the monumental gates of the 7-km city wall of ancient Halikarnassos — though "survivor" is generous for the two remaining stumps. The area includes a short section of wall foundations and is free to explore around the clock. Myndos, the ancient city at the end of the road west, now lies buried beneath the resort of Gümüşlük. In its heyday, Myndos had such grand gates that the philosopher Diogenes urged them to be shut before the entire town got out.

The Windmills stand on the ridge west of the bay in a line of seven, in various states of dilapidation. Used from the mid-18th century to the 1970s for grinding corn, the area is free to stroll and popular for sunset views over the bay. Similar windmills dot the peninsula beyond.

The Zeki Müren Art Museum at Zeki Müren Cd 19 celebrates the prolific, flamboyant singer-songwriter Zeki Müren (1931–96). He retired to Bodrum when his health failed, and his house has been turned into a museum displaying memorabilia — including his famously extensive costume collection. Open Tuesday to Sunday 08:30–17:30; adult admission 75 TL.

Other points of interest include the Kumbahçe Mosque on Atatürk Cd with its richly tiled interior, the Ottoman cemetery on Neyzen Tevfik Cd with interesting funerary monuments, and the marina which was once a shipyard known for building traditional gulets — two or three-masted wooden boats now used for leisure cruises. The ruins of Pedasa, a small city-state from 400 BC, can be reached up a dirt road and explored for free.

Beaches in central Bodrum are pebble but good for swimming. For sandy beaches, head west to Gümbet, 4 km from the center, or to Bitez just beyond. Camel Beach is a small resort strip further west where camels parade up and down the strand, offering rides that are as authentically overpriced as any in Arabia. A waterbus runs to Camel Beach from Bodrum Marina. Kara Ada ("Black Island") is a wooded, nearly uninhabited island popular on boat trips, with hot springs in a cave on its north coast — though they're lukewarm and sulphurous.

Scuba diving is well served, with half a dozen operators offering day trips for qualified divers from April to September. Dive sites include shipwrecks, a sunken Dakota DC-47, rock formations deepening into caverns around Kara Ada, and eelgrass meadows. Bodrum's clear calm waters also make it excellent for scuba training, including advanced courses. Windsurfing is better on the more exposed west coast around Turgutreis. Boat trips by modern launch or traditional gulet depart from the marina, with some operators picking up from resort beaches. The Bodrum Aquapark on Bekiroğlu Cd has 23 waterslides, a 350-meter wave river, a wave pool, and various other pools, open May to September. A hamam (Turkish bath) can be found at Fabrika Sk 48, open daily 07:00–23:00 with separate facilities for men and women. A Modern and Jazz Festival is held in April, and the Ballet Festival runs July to August.

Bodrum has a vivid nightlife scene. Körfez is a long-established rock bar on the waterfront at Cumhuriyet Cd 59. Funk is an open-air club just north, pricey and couples only, open nightly 21:00–04:00. Adamik near the castle is open 24 hours. Kule Rock City on Cumhuriyet Cd is a rock bar open 20:00–05:00. Club Catamaran east of the castle is built on a large catamaran with a glass-deck dance floor — at night the boat putters into the harbour where underwater lights attract fish to swim beneath the transparent floor, open nightly 21:30–03:00. Karnas Vineyards, 20 km northeast in the hills, offer wine tasting and a restaurant.

Bodrum's dining caters primarily to meat-lovers, though most places serve both halal food and alcohol. Körfez at Neyzen Tevfik Cd 2 is a quality seafood restaurant on the marina front (daily 09:00–23:00). Bodrum Kumru by Kumbahçe Mosque is cheap and cheerful with great value (daily 09:00–22:00). Umurça Pide Salonu at Derviş Görgün Cd 19 serves excellent sfiha — flatbread with mincemeat (daily 10:00–21:00). Berk Balık on Cumhuriyet Cd 167 is a friendly fish restaurant with castle views (daily 11:00–00:00). Sunger Pizza at Neyzen Tevfik Cd 160 has a varied menu beyond pizza (daily 08:00–23:00). In Gümbet, The Windy Bay off Melengeç Sk is good for inexpensive food, and Cafe De Colombia at Mister Hadi Sk 6 is a budget steakhouse open late. Always haggle at street markets — for clothing, aim for half the quoted price.

Accommodation ranges from budget pensions to luxury resorts. In the city center, Su Hotel on Turgut Reis Cd is a charming small hotel with a pool (B&B double from 2,000 TL). El Vino Hotel on Pamili Sk is a calm retreat on the north edge of town (B&B double 1,500 TL). Marmara Bodrum at Sulu Hasan Cd 18 sits on the hillside with charm (B&B double 2,500 TL). The Doubletree by Hilton at Neyzen Tevfik 168 is a smart, well-run marina hotel with excellent reviews (B&B double 2,500 TL). Budget options include Atrium Hotel (B&B double 1,000 TL), Hotel İstanköy on Atatürk Cd (B&B double 1,500 TL), and Kaya Pansiyon at Eski Hükümet Sk 10 for peaceful central rooms. In Gümbet, Green House Apart Hotel on Adnan Menderes Cd offers great value with pool and kitchenettes (double room only from 500 TL). Jasmine Beach Hotel on the beachfront east of the bay is good value (B&B double 1,500 TL). Asteria Bodrum Resort at Adnan Menderes Cd 63 delivers reliable comfort (B&B double 3,000 TL). For luxury, Torba has the Vogue Hotel (B&B double 6,000 TL) and Rixos Premium on the beachfront (B&B double 5,000 TL). The secluded Kempinski Hotel Barbaros Bay in Yalıçiftlik offers luxury 14 km southeast of town (B&B double 4,000 TL). Camping is available at Bodrum Ecofarm under orange and grapefruit trees from around 300 TL per person.

The Bodrum Peninsula is lined with resort villages, the largest being Turgutreis, Gümüşlük, and Yalıkavak. The Greek island of Kos lies 20 km southwest and is easily visited as a day trip by catamaran from Bodrum Marina. The ancient Greek ruins of Priene, Miletus, and Didyma are about 80 km north. The resort town of Kuşadası lies beyond, with the must-see ruins of Ephesus 20 km further inland. Datça, an unspoilt town south across the Gulf of Gökova, is reachable by ferry.
